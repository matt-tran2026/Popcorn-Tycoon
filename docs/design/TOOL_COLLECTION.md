## Split between gameplay and presentation

| Concern | Lives in | Who reads it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stats, rarity, behaviour family, price | `Config.Tools.<id>` (shared) | Server decides; client only previews targets |
| Model, grip, named points, trail | `ToolPresentation.<id>.model` (shared) | `ToolModels` (server) builds the Tool |
| Pose clips, timing markers | `ToolPresentation.<id>.clips` | `ToolAnimator` (client) |
| Sounds (uploaded id + fallback, volume, pitch range) | `ToolPresentation.<id>.sounds` | `ToolEffects.sound` (client) |
| Harvest VFX parameters | `ToolPresentation.<id>.vfx` | `ToolEffects` (client) |

`Config.validate` checks that every tool in `Config.Tools` has a presentation entry with the same rarity, clips whose keys run in order from 0 to 1, trails that name real points, and a Swing clip exactly `swingSeconds` long. Nothing in `ToolPresentation`, `ToolAnimator` or `ToolEffects` can change a harvest: effects play only for stalks the server lists in its `HarvestResult`.

Stable tool ids are PascalCase and match `Config.Tools` keys (`Sickle`, `LongScythe`, `WeedWhacker`, `ThunderRake`, `Flamethrower`, …). The catalog's "Bare Hands" is the existing `Manual` hand pick.

## Behaviour families

Tools combine shared harvesting families rather than having 36 separate scripts: SingleTarget, ArcSweep, LineHarvest, ContinuousHarvest, ProjectileHarvest, AreaOfEffect, ChainHarvest, DashHarvest, PullHarvest, InstantConversion, ChargedAttack, MultiPulseHarvest. `Types.ToolBehavior` lists the implemented ones: **ArcSweep**, **SingleTarget**, **LineHarvest**, **PullHarvest**, **DashHarvest**, **TargetedPull** and **ContinuousHarvest**, all through `ToolRules.pickTargets` (used by `HarvestService.swing` and the client's target glow). PullHarvest (Harvest Hook) uses the arc rule with a long, fairly narrow cone (14 studs, 60°, the 2 nearest ripe stalks), so it reaches rows two away without walking up to them; the pull itself is presentation (VFX below) and changes no reward. ArcSweep and SingleTarget need `arcDegrees`; SingleTarget must have maxTargets 1 and uses a narrow arc, the Corn Clippers' 90°. LineHarvest (Pitchfork) needs `laneWidth` instead: ripe stalks in a straight lane from `ToolRules.LaneBehind` (1 stud) behind the character to `reach` ahead, within half the lane width of the facing line, ordered by distance along the lane. `Config.validate` keeps the lane under two row gaps (Pitchfork: 4 studs, rows 5 apart), so one thrust only ever covers one row. With stalks 5 studs apart and 11 studs of reach, a full 3-stalk thrust needs the first stalk within 1 stud; that is the tool's positioning skill. Only swings harvest (creator's report, Nov 6): swings reach the server on the `ToolSwing` remote (`(toolId)` only), which ToolController fires only when its cooldown lets the swing animation play; ToolService.swing checks the tool is the one in the character's hand and calls HarvestService.swing. Nothing harvests on `Tool.Activated` any more: it replicated every click, and the server accepted clicks up to half a cooldown early that the client never animated (spam-clicking the Long Scythe for 4 s gave 4 visible swings but 5 harvests, out of step). After the fix the same spam gave 4 swings and 4 harvests at matching times. Hand picking is refused while a swung tool is equipped (HarvestService.attempt, "Swing your tool to harvest"), and PromptController zeroes the pick prompts' reach meanwhile (reach, not Enabled, because the server drives Enabled); putting the tool away brings them back. Swing cadence (every tool): an accepted swing sets the next due time one cooldown after the later of now and the previous due time, and a swing may arrive up to half a cooldown early. This absorbs network jitter at short cooldowns (Clippers 0.12 s) yet caps the rate (11 accepted in 1.2 s of per-frame spam). Each new family is added to HarvestService with its own server checks (ownership, equip, cooldown, target cap, dedup, backpack), then listed in `Types.ToolBehavior`. Alternating hands (Twin Sickles, Nov 6): an ArcSweep tool with `sideDegrees` and `combo` swings its hands in turn. Each hand's arc (110°) is centred `sideDegrees` (30°) toward that hand, so the right hand covers 85° right to 25° left of facing. Every `combo.every`-th (3rd) successful swing in a row, each within `combo.windowSeconds` (1.6 s) of the last hit, is a Combo Sweep with `combo.arcDegrees` (360°) at the same reach and target cap (4). A miss (nothing cut) or a pause ends the streak; that is the tool's positioning weakness. The rule is pure and shared (`ToolRules.nextSwing`/`afterSwing`). HarvestService keeps the per-player state (`hand`, `streak`, `lastHit`) for the tool it was counted with, advances it on every swing past the cadence check, and reports `hand`, `combo` and `streak` in every `HarvestResult`. ToolController predicts the same state, so the right clip plays at once and the target glow shows the next arc (the full circle when the combo is ready), and resyncs from each reply. Aimed tools (Corn Lasso, Nov 6): **TargetedPull** is the first family where the client says where it aimed. `ToolSwing` carries `(toolId, aim)`: the aim is the position of the ripe stalk the player pointed at (ToolController's `lassoAim`: the camera ray through the mouse, the last touch, or the screen centre in first person, picks the ripe stalk whose middle passes closest to it, within 3.5 studs of the ray and the tool's reach). The server treats it only as a hint: `ToolRules.lassoTargets` snaps it to one of the player's own ripe stalks within `AimSnap` (2.5) studs, that stalk must lie within reach (25) of the server's root plus `ReachSlack` (2, for lag), and anything else (not a finite Vector3, off every stalk, unripe, out of range) takes nothing ("Aim at ripe corn in range"). With no aim at all the primary is the nearest ripe stalk in an `AimCone` (40°) ahead, the same rule the client uses when nothing is under the cursor. The loop then snags the nearest other ripe stalks within `loopRadius` (5.5: one step on the 5-stud grid) of the primary and within reach, up to `maxTargets` (2), primary first. The client turns the character to face the throw and sends the request at the Swing clip's `Throw` marker with the aim taken at the click. Never more than 2 a throw is the weakness. InstantConversion must route to popcorn through the same value rules as the microwave and never add raw corn (AGENTS.md).

## Models (Blender → Roblox)

- Folder per tool: `assets/tools/<Rarity>/<Tool>/` with `scripts/` (build script, re-runnable), `source/<Tool>.blend`, `exports/<Tool>.fbx`, `audio/`, `previews/`. Shared art is in `assets/tools/_shared/`.
- 1 Blender unit = 1 stud. The grip point is the origin, the handle runs along +X toward the head, and a flat head lies in the X/Y plane.
- Colours are vertex colours on one white material, as for the earlier Blender assets. Each part that needs its own Roblox Material (wood, metal) is a separate mesh. Moving parts (fans, jaws, gears) will be separate meshes too.
- Triangle and texture budgets by rarity are on the design canvas (Common 1.5k up to Mythic and Secret 8k). Sickle: handle 240 and blade 544 triangles.
- Import: Studio 3D Importer with Upload on. The importer reads these FBX files at **100×** (as centimetres) and turns them 180°: scale the imported Model by 0.01 (`model:ScaleTo(model:GetScale() * 0.01)`). The turn does not matter, because the tool frame is derived from the meshes. Put the imported Model at `ServerStorage.PopcornTemplates.Tools.<template>` (place file only) and save the place. `ToolModels.build` finds the handle mesh by name and works out the tool frame from the meshes: X along the handle's long axis toward the head, Y the head's thinnest axis, Z the side the head sits on. That frame gives `Tool.Grip` and the named attachments, however the importer centred or turned the meshes. It sets the parts white, applies Materials and welds them. Without the template, or if its meshes are missing, the procedural part-built model in `ToolModels` is used and a warning is printed.

Holder grip and sliding parts (Nov 5, Pitchfork): `model.holder = true` makes the Tool's Handle a small invisible part at the grip point, so `joints` may name the handle mesh itself. The Pitchfork's shaft hangs off it on a `Thrust` Motor6D (pivot at the grip), the head is welded to the shaft, and named points and the trail ride on the shaft. Pose entries for tool joints may carry a translation in studs as entries 4–6 (`Thrust = { 0, 0, 0, 1.9, 0, 0 }` slides the fork 1.9 studs along tool X), so a thrust can move the tool through the hand, which rotations alone cannot. `ToolModels.toolFrame` also accepts a head centred on the handle (no side to point Z at): Z is then X × Y.

Moving parts (Nov 5, Corn Clippers): `model.joints` names meshes that get a Motor6D from the Handle (named after the joint, e.g. `Jaw`) turning about the tool frame's Y axis through a pivot point. `model.welds` welds a mesh to another mesh instead of the Handle (the upper handle rides on the jaw). `model.head` names the flat mesh whose thinnest axis defines Y, needed when other head meshes are not flat (springs, bolts). Pose clips drive a joint by name (`Jaw = { 0, -24, 0 }`): `ToolAnimator` treats any pose name that is not a character joint as a Motor6D inside the held Tool. Procedural stand-ins must be built in the tool frame with the Handle's X pointing at the head, and express `Grip` and points in the Handle's frame. A Tool carries `ModelSource` ("Blender" or "Procedural") and `Rarity` attributes.

## Animation

Roblox animations made in Blender cannot simply be dropped onto an R15 avatar, and published animation ids need the creator's upload. Tool animations are therefore **pose clips in code**. `ToolAnimator` rotates R15 joints (Waist, Neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists) on top of what the Animator plays, once per frame from one PreSimulation connection. The legs keep walking, and rotations never pile up even when nothing else drives a joint. R6 gets the shoulders and neck. Clips use keys at fractions 0–1 with Sine, Back, Quad or Linear easing, plus named markers (`Hit`, `Shing`) that time sounds and effects. Per tool: `Equip`, `Idle` (loop) and the action clips, with Overheat, Charge and the like for the families that need them. Keyframed Animation assets remain an option later for full-body moves (Pogo Plunger, Rocket Plow) once uploads are possible.

The hold is `ToolPresentation.hold(forward, face)` in the hand's grip frame (−Z forward, +Y up). The Sickle points its handle forward and a little up with the blade flat (`hold((0, 0.3, −1), (0, −1, 0))`). The old Grip stood the blade upright over the head, hiding the face. A tool may add a `SwingAlt` clip of the same length; ToolController alternates it with `Swing` so rapid repeats differ (Corn Clippers: wrist flick up, then down, with the jaw snapping 24° shut at the Hit marker). Pitchfork clips: Equip 0.36 s (swung up from the hip, Shing at 60%); Idle 2.6 s with a tiny bob of the shaft; Swing (the thrust) 0.42 s: pull back with the shaft sliding 1 stud back (33%), stab with the shaft 1.9 studs forward (50%, Hit at 48%), hold, recover. Keep body yaw small (waist ≤ 8°): a 20° twist in the first playtest swept the prongs about 4 studs across the lane, so it read as a swing rather than a stab. Snips run 0.06 s apart down the row, nearest first. Harvest Hook clips (holder grip, `Reach` motor on the leather grip): Equip 0.34 s; Idle 2.5 s; Swing 0.42 s in three beats: reach (arm up, hook cocked back, grip 0.4 back, 19%), hook (throw forward with the grip lunging 1.6 studs out, Hit at 45% = 0.19 s), pull (yank back with the grip 0.6 behind the hand, 64%), settle. Twin Sickles clips (Nov 6): the R15 default tool hold raises the right arm straight forward (measured in Play: RightShoulder (79.2, 0, −6.3), RightElbow (9.8, 0.3, 5.4)); nothing holds the left arm, so every Twin Sickles key merges a guard pose that mirrors it (`LeftShoulder (79, 0, 6)`, `LeftElbow (10, 0, −5)`; a mirror flips Y and Z). Pose angles are applied before a joint's base pose, so on an arm held forward the shoulder's **Z** swings it sideways (+Z toward the character's right) and the wrist's Z flicks the hand; Y only rolls it. Held joints (creator, Nov 6): layered on the run animation, the left arm still swung with every stride while the right stayed in the tool hold, so `ToolPresentation.<id>.holdJoints` (Twin Sickles: LeftShoulder, LeftElbow, LeftWrist) makes ToolAnimator write those joints from identity at full weight, ignoring the Animator (R15 only; R6 keeps layering). Running, both hands now move the same 0.1 studs relative to the torso. Swing (right) and SwingAlt (left) are 0.3 s mirror images (cock out with a 20° waist twist, whip across, Hit at 42%); Combo is 0.45 s: a wind-up, then the R15 `Root` joint (added to ToolAnimator; R6 skips it) turns the whole body 360° with both arms flung out (Spin marker 16%, Hit 45%). Sickle clips: Equip 0.32 s (hand drawn up from the hip, wrist flourish, Shing at 55%); Idle 2.4 s sway; Swing 0.32 s (cock right with a waist twist, whip left, follow-through, settle; Hit at 42%, so harvest snaps start 0.13 s after the server confirms).

## Sound

Original sounds only, synthesised with Python (numpy and scipy) by each tool's `scripts/synth_*.py` with a fixed seed: 44.1 kHz mono 16-bit WAV peaking at −3 dBFS. The game varies pitch within each sound's `pitch` range. Volumes follow the canvas mix: equip around 0.3, actions around 0.35, harvest hits around 0.5. Harvest sounds are 3D at the stalk; equip and swing play for the holder. Sounds with no uploaded id use `fallback` (the old swing and snip) or stay silent.

Uploading audio: the Studio MCP can only upload images, so audio goes through Studio's Asset Manager, either by the creator or by Claude with desktop control (done this way for the Sickle on Nov 5). Steps: Asset Manager → Import, set the file-type filter to Audio Files, choose the WAVs in `assets/tools/<Rarity>/<Tool>/audio/`, confirm the rights/0-Robux dialog, copy each asset id (check it with `MarketplaceService:GetProductInfo`), then set `id = "rbxassetid://…"` in `ToolPresentation.<id>.sounds` and in the manifest. Never invent ids.

## VFX

`ToolEffects` keeps pooled effects under `workspace.Terrain`: one leaf `ParticleEmitter` on one Attachment, moved and told to `Emit` for each burst. A swing creates no Parts or emitters, and 10 swings in a row still leave one emitter. Each burst is full under 60 studs from the camera, halved to 120 and skipped beyond. `ToolEffects.particleScale` is the hook for a future reduced-effects setting. The leaf texture is original (drawn with PIL, `_shared/make_leaf.py`) and uploaded as `rbxassetid://107437834728710`. Swing trails are a `SwingTrail` between two named points on the handle, switched on for 80% of the swing. Tools with `vfx.snipFlash` also get a tiny white sparkle at each confirmed cut, from one pooled `PopcornToolFlash` emitter (built-in `rbxasset://textures/particles/sparkles_main.dds`, nothing to upload; skipped beyond 60 studs).

Hook Pull (`vfx.pull`, Harvest Hook, Nov 5): at the Hit marker each confirmed stalk gets a thin streak from the hook's `HookCrown` point (`ToolEffects.hookLine`, up to 3 pooled Beams, 0.12 s), then its ear flies back to the player (`ToolEffects.pullEar`). Ears are part-built (Pebble cob and tip, two husk leaves, a gold Trail), at most 6 pooled in a local `workspace.PopcornToolFx` folder; a pull with every ear busy is skipped. Each flies a quadratic curve from the stalk to the player's chest in 0.36 s, bowing 3.2 studs up and 1.8 sideways (alternating sides so two ears split), and spins as it comes. The end point follows the player. One Heartbeat connection moves all ears with `BulkMoveTo` and disconnects when none fly. On landing: a sparkle and the `Pull` sound. This brings back flying corn for this tool only: the old kernel arcs stay removed for the other tools (creator, Nov 3); the Hook Pull is the hook's catalog ability.

Dual wield and Combo Sweep (Twin Sickles, Nov 6): `model.offhand` makes ToolModels build a copy of the finished Tool (meshes, points, trail recoloured green) as an `Offhand` model. On each server-side Equipped it is cloned into the Tool and welded to `LeftHand` (`LeftGrip`, C0 = `LeftGripAttachment`, R6 falls back to the classic grip offset); unequip or tool removal destroys it. Two fixes from the creator's first look (Nov 6): the copy lives **inside the Tool**, because in first person Roblox's camera hides every character part outside a Tool; and its parts are held by fixed **Welds** (offsets measured when the copy is built), because WeldConstraints in a copy that the LeftGrip weld snaps into place in the same step kept the wrong offset and left the blade about 110 studs away. ToolController looks for the right trail outside the copy. Every client sees both blades. Only one sickle is modelled: the mesh is symmetric about its flat plane, so `ToolPresentation.mirrorHold` (forward mirrored, face flipped) turns the same meshes into a true mirror image. ToolController lights only the swinging hand's trail (right blue, left green; both on the Combo). At the Combo clip's Spin marker `ToolEffects.comboSweep` whirls two pooled Trail ribbons (blue and green, half a turn apart, radius 3.4, width 1.5) once round the player in 0.3 s. One Heartbeat connection does this, and a new combo restarts it.

Wind trail (Long Scythe, Nov 6): at the Swing clip's `Sweep` marker `ToolEffects.windSweep` sweeps one pooled Trail ribbon per `vfx.wind.radii` entry (6 and 11.5 studs, at most 3) through `degrees` (180) from the player's right, across the front, to their left in 0.2 s (ease out), so players see the 15-stud reach. Its own pool and one Heartbeat connection, like the combo spinners. The two-handed hold is `holdJoints` on the left arm with a base pose (`twoHand` in ToolPresentation) reaching across to the butt end; the shaft is held across the body with the blade hanging down from its top (a flat-lying blade was edge-on to the camera and vanished). The sweep's follow-through stays short (shoulder -38, waist 28) because the shaft already points left; a bigger one carried the blade round behind the player.

Precision Cutting (Hedge Trimmer, Nov 6): LineHarvest with a 6-stud lane (`laneWidth`), reach 8, 5 targets, 0.75 s. Walking down the gap between two rows puts both rows in the lane; standing on a row covers that row only; nothing more than 3 studs to the side (the weakness). On the 5-stud grid a lane from `LaneBehind` (1) to 8 ahead holds 2 stalks per row, so a trim cuts at most 4 (the catalog's cap of 5 is kept; open decision). Effects (`vfx.trim`): at the Swing clip's `Hit` marker `ToolEffects.trimStrip` lays one pooled Neon part the size of the lane on the ground (found by a short raycast down from the root) and fills it from the back edge outward in 0.18 s, then fades it over 0.35 s; it stays where it was laid. While the blades run, `ToolEffects.bladeSpray` switches on two emitters made once per tool inside the `BladeMid` (grass flecks, the shared Leaf_Fleck texture) and `BladeRoot` (small orange sparks) attachments for 0.32 s. No swing trail: the cut is a rectangle, not an arc. Blade chatter is a new clip field, `buzz` (`ToolAnimator.addBuzz`): between two clip fractions each listed joint gets its value times sin(2π·hz·t) added, fading over the last 15%; the trimmer's two blade meshes slide ±0.07 studs at 22 Hz on their own motors (`BladeA`, `BladeB`, translation entries 4–6), in opposite directions, and the right wrist jitters ±1.4°. Off-centre grips: `model.gripSide` moves the grip point along tool Z from the handle mesh's bounding-box centre (the trimmer's rear loop is a closed loop whose centre sits 0.43 studs below the grip bar). For this tool `ToolModels.toolFrame` points Z from the housing toward the bar (the other meshes sit 0.07 studs below the loop's centre on average), so Blender (x, y, z) maps to tool (x, z, −y); `build_hedge_trimmer.py` reports that margin as `sideMargin`.

Long-Range Retrieval (Corn Lasso, Nov 6): `vfx.lasso`. At the `Throw` marker ToolController hides the held loop (`LocalTransparencyModifier` on the `LassoLoop` mesh, or the part-built pieces marked `LassoLoopPiece`) and `ToolEffects.lassoThrow` flies a rope loop from the `RopeOut` point to the aimed ear (or the ground ahead for a throw at nothing) at 140 studs/s on a slight arc, opening to 1.25 studs and spinning 1.5 turns. Each rope is 10 pooled rope-coloured cylinder segments laid round a ring each frame (resized as it opens and cinches), a knot and a Beam from the lasso to the knot that sags in flight and tightens on the way back; at most 3 ropes (throws overlap: the cycle is about 0.6 to 0.9 s against a 0.4 s cooldown). The loop hovers over the ear until the server's answer (`ToolEffects.lassoReply`; ToolController matches a harvest to the waiting rope aimed nearest its first stalk, a refusal to the oldest), at most 0.35 s. For a harvest it cinches to 0.34 studs in 0.1 s (Harvest sound, leaf burst and sparkle per stalk), snagged ears hop into the loop behind the aimed one, and the ears (the Hook Pull's pool) ride the rope back to the hand in 0.34 s; the Pull sound and a sparkle play on the catch. A refused throw reels back an empty loop. One Heartbeat connection with `BulkMoveTo`, disconnected when no rope is out. The `Loop` motor turns the held loop in its own plane: a small sway on Idle and one full twirl overhead during the wind-up; the clip sets it back to 0 while the loop is hidden, so the reverse turn is never seen.

Spring Dash (Spring-Loaded Picker, Nov 6): **DashHarvest**, the first family that moves the player. The harvest is the LineHarvest lane (`ToolRules.usesLane`: 6 wide, 1 behind to 12 ahead, 4 nearest), cut short where the character would hit something: `ToolRules.dashReach` sweeps a body-sized box (2 × 3.4 × 1, from knee to head height) along the flat facing with `workspace:Blockcast` (RespectCanCollide, every character ignored) and stops 1.5 studs short of the first hit. The server runs it from its own root part and passes the result to `pickTargets` (`reach` argument) and reports it as `HarvestResult.dashReach`; the client runs the same cast to stop the lunge. The Swing clip (0.55 s) coils for 0.15 s; at its `Dash` marker ToolController sends `ToolSwing` (so the server's lane starts where the character takes off, not where the click happened) and lunges the local character: a `LinearVelocity` on the root attachment with per-axis force on X/Z only holds `dash.speed` (40) along the facing for reach / speed seconds (0.3 s), a `dash.hop` (20 studs/s, about 0.9 studs high) upward kick, then most of the speed is bled off (the character skids about 0.7 studs past: the overshoot weakness, together with no steering mid-dash). `Config.validate` makes sure a dash lands well inside one cooldown. Each confirmed stalk pops when the player reaches it (distance along the dash / speed) rather than on a stagger. Effects (`vfx.dash`): `ToolEffects.dashStreak` (one Trail per character between two root attachments, made once, switched on for the dash), `ToolEffects.dashDust` (one pooled smoke emitter, a ring of puffs at take-off and landing) and a sparkle at the claw. Model rig additions: a joint may hang from another moving mesh (`joints.<mesh>.on`; the jaws ride the Plunger rod), and `model.stretch` makes a mesh span from its own motor's pivot to a followed motor's slide: ToolModels marks it with `StretchAxis`/`StretchRest`, and `ToolAnimator.applyStretch` resizes it along tool X every frame and moves it half the slide (the coil squashes to 70% while the head pulls back 0.45 and stretches to 140% when it fires 0.6 out). Like every pose, this is local to the holder.

Continuous Cutting and Overheat (Weed Whacker, Nov 7): **ContinuousHarvest**, the first held tool. Nothing is swung: the client sends `ToolHold (toolId, holding)` when the button (click, tap or F) goes down and up, and the server does the rest. `ToolService.hold` checks the tool is in the character's hand (for a start; a stop is always accepted; starts are capped at 20 a second) and `HarvestService.hold` checks ownership, equip state, a living character and the overheat lock. One Heartbeat connection in ToolService runs every running cutter: `HarvestService.cutTick` settles the heat and, when a cut is due, cuts the ripe stalk the shared rule picks from the server's own root part (`ToolRules.continuousTargets`: every ripe stalk within `reach` (7) of the root, all round, ordered by flat distance from the cutting head `continuous.headAhead` (3) studs ahead, so stalks in front go first; one per tick). Cadence: the due time moves on by one cooldown (0.12 s) per cut and never less than half a cooldown past now, so the rate averages one cut per cooldown and never two within half of one; a tick with nothing in reach looks again half a cooldown later (measured: 26 cuts in 3.05 s with every stalk kept ripe). Successful cuts go to the client on `HarvestResult` like a swing's (tool and the one target, played with no lead); a refusal (nothing in reach, bag full) is said once per press. Farms refresh once per frame with any cut. Heat is pure and shared (`ToolRules.newHeat`/`heatAt`/`settleHeat`/`setRunning`, on `workspace:GetServerTimeNow()`): it rises 1/`runSeconds` (7 s) per second while running; at full heat the engine stops and is locked for `coolSeconds` (3 s) while the heat falls back to empty; letting go early cools it at the same rate (2 s bursts with 1 s pauses never lock). The server mirrors the state onto the Tool (`Heat` at `HeatAt`, `Running`, `OverheatUntil`) on every change, never per frame, and every client computes the current heat from those with the same rule. Putting the tool away, losing the character or the tool stops the engine. Holding the button through an overheat starts it again once cooled (the client re-sends the start). `HarvestService.swing` refuses held tools, so the swing remote cannot cut with them. On the 5-stud grid a 7-stud radius holds the 4 stalks around a gap (3.54 away; the next ring is 7.9 away) or the stalk underfoot and its 4 neighbours, so the player has to keep walking through the crop: that and the overheat are the weakness.

Effects (`vfx.whacker`, client, local to the holder like every pose): ToolController runs one RenderStepped session per equip. A press shows the engine starting at once; the server's `Running` wins if it has not confirmed within 0.3 s. Running swaps the idle clip for the looping `Cut` clip (a waist sweep of the head, wrists buzzing at 28 Hz: looping clips may now carry `buzz`, which no longer fades for a loop running to its end), `ToolAnimator.setSpin` turns the blade on its `Spin` motor (240 deg/s idling, 1260 running, eased up in 0.35 s and down in 0.8 s, stopped while locked) and `setShake` rattles the engine on its `Engine` motor (three fast incommensurate sines; both are layered after the clips every frame, on tool joints the clips never pose). The `Run` sound loops on the Handle with its playback speed following the blade (0.55 idling to 1.15) and is silent while locked. The `WhackerHeat` gauge mesh (white in the model, Neon) is tinted along `heatColors` (cool blue, amber, red) and flashes while locked; `ToolEffects.heatMeter` puts a small HEAT bar above the hotbar (COOLING while locked). The model's trail runs between two rim points that ride the spinning blade (`model.pointsOn`, new: named points attached to a moving mesh instead of the Handle), so it draws the spinning disc, on only while running; leaves spray from the head (`ToolEffects.whackerSpray`). A fresh `OverheatUntil` plays the `Overheat` clip (the head drops and is shaken), the cough-and-hiss sound, and a puff of smoke from `EngineTop` and sparks from `Exhaust` (`ToolEffects.overheatPuff`). Two-handed hold: both arms are held outright (`holdJoints` includes the right arm too), so their whole pose comes from the clips; the base pose and the grip come from a forward-kinematics search on the live R15 rig (right hand at the hip, left palm 0.22 studs from the loop's top bar; the loop moved from 1.3 to 0.85 along the shaft because the left hand could not reach it). The disc runs 0.3 to 0.6 studs above the ground.

## Collection and chest compatibility

Each tool has, or will have: stable id, display name, rarity, description (Config); icon and preview render (`previews/`); equip model (template); animation, sound and VFX references (ToolPresentation); gameplay config (Config). The planned tiered chests and a preview stage (the tool on a slow turntable over a rarity-coloured glow disc) can read these without new data. No chest system is implemented.

## Stage plan

Stage 1 is Sickle, Long Scythe, Weed Whacker, Thunder Rake and Flamethrower, then Common/Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic, Secret, and final integration (catalog order and pipeline on the design canvas). A tool is complete only with its model imported, its animations, sounds and VFX in game, its gameplay integrated and its tests run (manifest `status`).
